DOJ Withdraws California Ballot Lawsuit After Emergency Relief Fails

An election lawsuit can lose its requested remedy before the underlying factual dispute is resolved.

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 8, 2026

United States; Canadian public-law comparison

An election lawsuit can lose its requested remedy before the underlying factual dispute is resolved.

The U.S. Justice Department filed a notice of voluntary dismissal on November 6 in its California ballot-segregation case. The filing followed unsuccessful requests for emergency relief, including the Supreme Court’s November 2 denial.

The government had sought to separate mail ballots associated with voters it identified as likely noncitizens while eligibility was checked. That characterization remained disputed; the list did not establish that every person named was ineligible or had voted.

In its dismissal notice, DOJ argued that processing and commingling of ballots made the requested relief unavailable and left no remaining basis for the case to proceed. That is the government’s explanation for withdrawal - not a judicial finding resolving the list’s accuracy.

The development is a material update to the earlier emergency orders, rather than a repetition of those decisions.

For litigators, it demonstrates why remedy and timing must be assessed together. A requested order may cease to be workable as the underlying process advances.

The withdrawal establishes neither that all listed voters were citizens nor that they were noncitizens.

Ending the case does not answer the factual question that prompted it.

Sources: Notice of voluntary dismissal, Document 180; Original Reuters URL; Reuters syndication showing November 6 publication; Los Angeles Times report.
