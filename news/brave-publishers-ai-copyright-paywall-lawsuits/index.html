Publishers Sue Brave Over AI Content Supply and Alleged Paywall Bypass

AI copyright litigation is examining the businesses supplying content as well as the companies developing models.

AI copyright litigation is examining the businesses supplying content as well as the companies developing models.

The New York Times and, in a separate action, the New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune and Orlando Sentinel sued Brave Software in Manhattan federal court on November 2. The complaints contain allegations, not findings of infringement.

The Times alleges that Brave copied journalism into its search index, used it in AI answers and supplied material through its search API. It also challenges an alleged paywall-bypass feature. The reviewed complaint names Brave Software as defendant; references to AI customers do not make those companies defendants in this action.

For counsel advising content businesses or AI providers, the litigation highlights the need to map the entire supply chain.

Who collected the material? What access restrictions applied? What permissions were obtained? What did an intermediary supply, and what did the recipient ultimately display?

Those are separate factual questions. The presence of several services in one technical pipeline does not establish that each participant committed the same legal act.

Later November 3 reporting continued to seek Brave’s response. No merits ruling or substantive court response from Brave was identified in this review.

The European opinion issued today concerns a different legal regime and procedural stage. Pairing the stories can illuminate the technology, but neither determines the other’s outcome.

Sources: Reuters; Supplied complaint URL; Times complaint, primary copy; Separate publishers’ case documentation summary; November 3 follow-up reporting.

Verification note: Case numbers are 1:26-cv-09549 and 1:26-cv-09554. The Times complaint was directly reviewed; the second filing was corroborated through reporting and case-document summaries.
