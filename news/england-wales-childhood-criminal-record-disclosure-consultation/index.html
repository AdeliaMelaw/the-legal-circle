November 13, 2026

England and Wales

Open consultation - no rule change

A childhood conviction can surface years later when someone applies for a job or volunteer role. A Ministry of Justice consultation asks when disclosure still serves a safeguarding purpose and when it creates a barrier to rehabilitation.

The consultation opened on November 12, 2026, and applies to England and Wales. It proposes changes to what appears on different levels of criminal record checks. The proposals would require legislation and do not change what anyone must disclose today.

For standard and enhanced checks, the government proposes narrowing the list of childhood offences that require lifelong disclosure. It also proposes ending lifelong disclosure based on a childhood custodial sentence of two years or less. Those options must be read alongside the proposal to preserve lifelong disclosure for the most serious offences, including serious violent, sexual and terrorism-related offences.

For basic checks, the consultation looks at shortening or removing the rehabilitation period for convictions resulting in a fine and ending automatic disclosure of childhood out-of-court resolutions. It also considers changing the rules under which a later conviction can extend the period for which an earlier childhood conviction remains unspent. The government is not proposing changes to the Disclosure and Barring Service’s separate barring scheme.

The practical distinction is between holding a record, disclosing it on a particular check, and barring someone from particular work. A change to disclosure would not, by itself, erase police records or remove a bar. For Canadian youth justice and employment lawyers, the consultation offers a comparison: what information does a particular role actually require, and how long does a childhood offence remain relevant to that decision?

Sources: Ministry of Justice consultation page, published November 12 · Full consultation, CP 1703
