Canada Changes Low-Wage Temporary Foreign Worker Rules: What Applies Now?

Canada’s latest Temporary Foreign Worker Program announcement contains two different timelines. Employers should keep them separate.

Canada

Canada’s latest Temporary Foreign Worker Program announcement contains two different timelines. Employers should keep them separate.

Employment and Social Development Canada announced changes effective November 6, alongside regional measures proposed for March 1, 2027 and contingent on provincial or territorial participation.

Measure Announced status
Maximum employment duration associated with low-wage LMIA applications submitted from November 6 increases from one year to up to two years Effective November 6
Early childhood educators join exceptions to the refusal to process applications in metropolitan areas with unemployment of at least 6% Effective November 6
Regional occupation-based access framework Proposed for March 1, 2027, contingent on provincial or territorial opt-in
Increase from a 10% to 15% workforce cap for eligible employers in selected communities Proposed opt-in flexibility

These changes do not mean every application will succeed or every worker automatically receives a two-year work permit.

For immigration and employment counsel, the immediate task is to match the application date, occupation and location to the applicable requirements. Planning around the proposed regional framework requires a separate check of participation and implementation details.

Employers should also avoid treating an announced maximum as a guaranteed authorization.

A future hiring option belongs in a contingency plan until the conditions for using it are actually met.
