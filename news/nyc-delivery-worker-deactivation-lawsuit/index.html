Amazon, Uber and Instacart Challenge NYC Delivery-Worker Deactivation Law

Amazon, Uber and Instacart are challenging New York City’s rules governing when delivery platforms can remove workers’ access to their applications.

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 1, 2026

United States; Canadian platform-work comparison

Amazon, Uber and Instacart are challenging New York City’s rules governing when delivery platforms can remove workers’ access to their applications.

The federal lawsuit was filed October 28, according to the docket, and reported October 29. The companies also sought a preliminary injunction.

The filing date corrects the original brief, which described the action as filed October 29.

What the companies challenge

The dispute concerns procedural and substantive restrictions on delivery-worker deactivation.

The companies argue that the law interferes with their contractual rights and raise constitutional objections. Those are allegations in a new lawsuit, not conclusions accepted by the court.

The issue is broader than a simple notice period: the challenge also concerns the grounds and process governing removal from a platform.

Subsequent October 30 update: the city responds

New York City’s Department of Consumer and Worker Protection defended Local Law 34 in an October 30 statement.

The department characterized the law as protecting safety and due process and said it would continue defending delivery-worker protections. That is the city’s position, not a ruling on the companies’ claims.

What remains undecided

No merits decision or injunction outcome was identified in the sources reviewed through October 31. The publicly accessible docket reviewed was last retrieved October 29, so it should not be treated as a complete real-time record.

For Canadian employment and platform-regulation lawyers, the case offers a comparison on procedural protections for people whose earning opportunities depend on access to an app.

The key questions concern the scope of municipal authority, the platform’s contractual discretion and the process required before access ends. Each will need to be assessed against the actual law and litigation record.

Sources: Reuters report · Case docket · City’s October 30 response
