Latham Sets Aside US$1 Million for Associates’ AI Contributions in 2027

When an associate builds an AI workflow that a whole practice group can use, who gets credit for the work? Latham & Watkins has put a dollar figure behind that question: US$1 million for its 2027 Associate AI Impact Award pool.

Legal Business reported the initiative on November 10, citing the firm’s statement that day. Reuters followed on November 11. The firm says the programme will recognize associates who lead or materially contribute to AI initiatives that benefit clients, matter teams, practice groups or the firm. A nomination and review process is planned, and individual awards will vary with the contribution’s impact and potential for wider use. The reported figure is the total pool, not a payment to any one associate or a record of awards already paid.

For law firm leaders, the interesting question is what an award should measure. A tool that cuts drafting time might look impressive until someone accounts for the extra verification it requires. A less visible change - such as making a review process more consistent across teams - might deliver more durable value.

A firm considering its own programme could ask nominees to show three things: what changed in the workflow, how the team verified the output, and whether the improvement can be repeated on other matters. Credit should also reflect who built, tested and maintained the process, especially when several people contributed.

Latham’s announcement establishes the incentive. Whether it produces better work for clients is a separate question, and one the programme has yet to answer.

Sources: Legal Business, November 10 · Reuters, November 11
