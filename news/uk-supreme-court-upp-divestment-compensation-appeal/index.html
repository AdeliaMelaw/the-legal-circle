United Kingdom

Hearing - awaiting judgment

A government can order an investor to sell a business on national-security grounds. If the investor says the forced sale produced less than market value, who should bear the difference? The UK Supreme Court heard that question on November 10, 2026, in a case arising from the divestment of telecoms company Upp.

In L1T FM Holdings UK Limited and another v. Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster, the investors challenged a final order under the National Security and Investment Act 2021. After Upp was sold, they sought damages for the difference between the price achieved and what they contend was its market value. The Administrative Court dismissed the claim, and the Court of Appeal dismissed the appeal. The Supreme Court case page listed the matter as awaiting judgment when checked November 12.

The question before the Court is whether a deprivation of property in the public interest can comply with Article 1 of Protocol 1 to the European Convention on Human Rights without paying market value or ensuring a way to recover it, where doing so would not undermine the policy objective. Participating counsel’s chambers reported the hearing on November 10.

For transaction counsel, the case puts the mechanics of a forced sale in focus. The power to require divestment and the ability to recover value from that divestment are different risks. A deal team assessing regulatory exposure should ask both how a sale could be required and what evidence would be available to show the effect of any restrictions on price.

No Supreme Court ruling has determined that the investors are owed compensation or what Upp’s market value was.
