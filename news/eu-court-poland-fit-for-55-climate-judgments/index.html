European Union

Judgments - annulment actions dismissed

Can the effect of an EU climate measure on a country’s energy choices change the voting rule needed to adopt it? In seven judgments delivered on November 12, 2026, the Court of Justice of the European Union held that the measures challenged by Poland could be adopted through the ordinary legislative procedure. It dismissed all seven annulment actions.

The cases covered land use and forestry, CO₂ standards for new cars and vans, the emissions trading market stability reserve, national emissions reduction targets, emissions trading for buildings and road transport, the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, and energy efficiency. The judgments leave the challenged measures in place.

The voting dispute turned on the measures’ legal basis. Under the ordinary legislative procedure, the European Parliament and Council legislate together, with the Council acting by qualified majority. Poland argued that exceptions requiring unanimity applied. The Court treated those exceptions narrowly. It held that none of the seven measures had as its primary purpose a significant change to a member state’s choice of energy sources and the general structure of its energy supply. It classified six measures under EU environmental policy and treated the energy-efficiency directive separately under the treaty’s energy provisions.

That distinction matters because legislation can have substantial economic consequences without those consequences determining its legal basis. For lawyers following EU climate litigation, the useful question is how the objective and content of each measure connect to the treaty provision used to adopt it. For Canadian exporters following the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, the narrower takeaway is that Poland’s challenge did not remove the measure. Importers and exporters still need to assess their obligations under the applicable rules and timetable.

Sources: Court of Justice release No. 163/26, with all seven case numbers
