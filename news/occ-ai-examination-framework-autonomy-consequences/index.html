OCC’s AI Framework Focuses on What Systems Can Do, Not Just Their Sophistication

A simple automated tool can create serious consequences if it is allowed to act. A sophisticated model confined to an internal task may present a different supervisory concern.

A simple automated tool can create serious consequences if it is allowed to act. A sophisticated model confined to an internal task may present a different supervisory concern.

That distinction is central to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency’s November 4 examination framework, titled “Super Intelligence: Supervisory Framework.”

The OCC says examiners should tailor their work to a use case’s materiality, consequences, autonomy and oversight. It also says the technology should not be treated as a separate risk category.

Guidance for examiners, not a new banking rule

The bulletin expressly states that it creates no new regulatory expectations, enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements for banks. It is a resource for examiners, and banks are not expected to use it to assess their own technology or risk management.

That boundary belongs prominently in any legal or procurement briefing.

The document may illuminate supervisory thinking without becoming an independent source of mandatory duties.

What counsel can take from it

As a practical analytical exercise, counsel can distinguish a system’s technical capabilities from its authority within the business.

Can it initiate an action? What limits apply? Who reviews exceptions? What happens when an output is wrong?

Those questions help connect technical descriptions to operational consequences. They also encourage teams to explain controls in terms a supervisor can assess.

The OCC says it intends to update the framework as the technology and supervisory understanding evolve.

The development is a new examination resource - not a new standalone AI compliance regime.
