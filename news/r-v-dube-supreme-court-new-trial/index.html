Supreme Court Confirms New Trial in R. v. Dubé

The Supreme Court of Canada has confirmed a new sexual-assault trial in R. v. Dubé, 2026 SCC 36.

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 3, 2026

Canada

The Supreme Court of Canada has confirmed a new sexual-assault trial in R. v. Dubé, 2026 SCC 36.

A majority dismissed the accused’s appeal on November 2, leaving the Quebec Court of Appeal’s new-trial order in place. Justice Côté dissented. The result is a new trial, not a conviction.

According to the Supreme Court’s public summary, the appellate dispute concerned the trial judge’s treatment of an honest-but-mistaken-belief-in-consent defence. The Quebec majority found that the judge had not identified the reasonable steps taken to ascertain consent, preventing a valid appellate assessment of the acquittal.

The important distinction for criminal counsel is between identifying the procedural result and explaining its full doctrinal reach.

The Court’s summary confirms that the appeal failed. It is not itself the Court’s reasons for judgment. At the time of review, the summary continued to indicate that a finalized print version of the oral judgment would follow.

Until that text is available, broader claims about new rules governing Crown appeals or deficient trial reasons would be premature.

For practitioners monitoring the case, the next step is to compare the finalized reasons with the Quebec judgments, particularly the boundary between an error of law and disagreement over factual assessment.

For now, the reportable outcome is narrow and consequential: the acquittal has not been restored, and the prosecution must be tried again.

Sources: Supreme Court Case in Brief; Hearing and oral-judgment notice; Supreme Court case docket.
