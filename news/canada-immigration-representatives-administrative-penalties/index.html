Canada Introduces Federal Penalties for Immigration Representatives

Paid immigration and citizenship representatives now face a federal administrative penalty system operating alongside professional discipline and criminal enforcement.

Legal Developments

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 1, 2026

Canada

Paid immigration and citizenship representatives now face a federal administrative penalty system operating alongside professional discipline and criminal enforcement.

Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada announced on October 28 that the rules were in force. They apply to authorized paid representatives, including lawyers and licensed consultants, as well as people providing paid services without authorization.

What conduct the system covers

IRCC identifies unauthorized paid representation, knowing involvement in misrepresentation and failure to provide required inspection documents.

Its announcement lists $5,000 for unauthorized paid representation and $15,000 for knowingly providing or helping provide false or misleading information.

Those figures should not be presented as the inevitable final penalty in every case. IRCC’s guide describes a calculation involving baseline amounts, impact, financial advantage and previous violations.

Response and review rights

The process includes a preliminary notice and an opportunity to respond. IRCC’s guide addresses situations in which a representative cannot contest alleged facts without disclosing privileged information.

A recipient of a notice of violation can request a review within 30 days. The guide states that combined penalties can reach $1.5 million per notice.

Public disclosure comes later

IRCC says it will publish names and violation details as penalties are imposed. Its online registry is expected in 2027.

The announcement does not establish misconduct by any particular representative. It also does not mean that the planned registry is already available.

For immigration practices, the immediate significance is an additional federal compliance process with financial and reputational consequences. Authority to practise remains essential, but authorization alone does not remove exposure for specified misconduct.

Timing: Rules announced as effective October 28; included in the October 30 catch-up because of subsequent reporting. Final regulations are scheduled for Canada Gazette, Part II publication on November 4.

Sources: IRCC announcement · IRCC compliance and review guide
