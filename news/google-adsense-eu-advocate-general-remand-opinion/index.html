Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 13, 2026

European Union

Nonbinding Advocate General opinion

When a competition authority alleges that a network of contracts can shut out rivals, how closely must it examine the duration of each agreement? That question is at the centre of Advocate General Juliane Kokott’s November 12, 2026 opinion in Commission v. Google and Alphabet, Case C‑826/24 P.

The dispute concerns Google’s AdSense for Search service, which allowed publishers to display ads alongside search results on their own sites. The European Commission found that contractual restrictions imposed between 2006 and 2016 amounted to an abuse of dominance and imposed a fine of nearly €1.5 billion in 2019. In September 2024, the General Court annulled the Commission’s decision after finding shortcomings in its examination of the circumstances, including the duration of the contractual relationships.

Kokott considers that the General Court demanded too detailed an examination of individual contracts and did not adequately weigh duration alongside the other evidence. In her view, the Commission can in principle assess the significant contracts cumulatively when evaluating their potential to exclude rivals. That does not make contract terms irrelevant. It puts them in the context of how the agreements worked across the market.

The Advocate General recommends setting aside the 2024 judgment and returning the case to the General Court. Her opinion is nonbinding. A referral would leave unresolved arguments for the General Court to examine, including challenges to the amount of the fine. The opinion did not reinstate the fine, and I found no Court of Justice judgment on the appeal by the November 13 cutoff.

For competition counsel, the evidence question cuts both ways: what does the contract network show about market access, and which variations in duration or termination rights could change that picture?

Sources: Court of Justice release No. 164/26 · Reuters, November 12
