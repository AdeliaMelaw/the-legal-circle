JD.com’s CECONOMY Deal Receives Conditional EU Approval

Regulatory clearance can be the beginning of a company’s compliance work.

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 3, 2026

European Union; Canadian cross-border transactions

Regulatory clearance can be the beginning of a company’s compliance work.

JD.com’s acquisition of CECONOMY, the owner of MediaMarkt and Saturn, received conditional EU approval on November 3. CECONOMY says this satisfies the transaction’s final outstanding regulatory condition. Approval is not completion: the company’s announcement anticipates closing in the coming days, while Reuters reports a target of November 12.

The Foreign Subsidies Regulation clearance includes remedies addressing the combined business’s operations. An official-statement report identifies equivalent terms for third-party retailers using JD.com’s warehousing and logistics services.

For deal counsel, that shifts attention from securing permission to demonstrating compliance after closing. An operational commitment needs people, records and workable commercial processes behind it.

The practical questions include who owns each obligation, how comparable service terms will be documented, and how disputes over access will be escalated. Those questions belong in integration planning, rather than being postponed until a regulator asks for evidence.

Canadian companies considering EU acquisitions should also distinguish the relevant review processes. CECONOMY separately identifies foreign-investment approvals alongside this foreign-subsidies clearance.

The immediate development is therefore conditional clearance, with completion still prospective in the reviewed sources.

A successful approval strategy must leave the business capable of performing what its lawyers promised.

Sources: Commission announcement, Danish; Commission announcement, English; CECONOMY announcement; Reuters; MLex official-statement report.
