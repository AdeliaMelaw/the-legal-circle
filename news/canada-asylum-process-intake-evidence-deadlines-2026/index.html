Canada’s New Asylum Process Takes Effect: Intake and Evidence Deadlines Must Be Kept Separate

Canada’s asylum-process changes are now in force. For lawyers, the immediate task is to distinguish the steps in a claim rather than treat every new deadline as one clock.

Analysis

Published by The Legal Circle

November 4, 2026

Canada

Canada’s asylum-process changes are now in force. For lawyers, the immediate task is to distinguish the steps in a claim rather than treat every new deadline as one clock.

IRCC announced that the regulations took effect on November 3, introducing a common online application process for inland and port-of-entry claims. The department describes faster processing as an expected benefit, not an outcome already demonstrated.

What changes at intake?

For inland claims, IRCC describes an initial portal submission indicating an intention to claim, followed by an in-person appointment where the claim is formally made. For claims eligible for referral, its backgrounder specifies 60 days to submit the completed application and required documents, with a one-time 30-day extension on request.

That period must not be confused with disclosure deadlines after referral to the Refugee Protection Division.

A separate post-referral deadline

An updated IRB practice notice establishes the following framework for personal documents:

Referral date Personal-document deadline
On or before November 2, 2026 Generally 10 days before the hearing
November 3, 2026-January 3, 2027 60 days after referral, through an automatic transitional extension
January 4, 2027 onward 30 days after referral

Country-conditions evidence remains subject to a separate hearing-based deadline. Later personal documents require the applicable late-disclosure process.

What counsel should record

A useful file chronology should distinguish the portal submission, formal claim, eligibility determination, referral and document deadlines. An extension available at one stage should not be assumed to extend another.

IRCC says previously submitted claims will be processed as usual. That does not eliminate the need to examine the IRB’s specific transitional provisions for a particular procedural step.

The practical priority is an accurate chronology. A more uniform intake system still requires precise advice about which deadline governs which document.

Sources: IRCC announcement; IRCC backgrounder; IRB practice notice; IRCC claimant instructions; Law360 Canada.
