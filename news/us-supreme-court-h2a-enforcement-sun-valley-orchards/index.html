U.S. Supreme Court Weighs Who Can Decide H-2A Penalties and Back Wages

Oral argument - decision pending

When the U.S. Department of Labor seeks back wages and civil penalties from a farm that participates in the H-2A temporary agricultural worker program, can the department adjudicate the case itself? Or must it ask a federal court to decide?

The U.S. Supreme Court heard argument on November 10, 2026, in Department of Labor v. Sun Valley Orchards, No. 25-966. The dispute follows an administrative proceeding involving more than US$550,000 in penalties and back wages. The Third Circuit ruled for the farm on the constitutional issue. The Supreme Court’s docket recorded the hearing but showed no decision in the November 12 check.

The justices agreed to hear two distinct questions. First, does Article III of the U.S. Constitution prevent the department from adjudicating these monetary claims? Second, does the H-2A statute itself, at 8 U.S.C. § 1188(g)(2), authorize it to do so? Both questions cover employment conditions for H-2A workers and domestic workers in corresponding employment.

The department connects the enforcement proceeding to the federal government’s authority over a program that permits employers to hire temporary foreign workers. The farm disputes that connection as a basis for agency adjudication. The case tests how the public-rights analysis from SEC v. Jarkesy applies when an agency seeks money for violations of conditions attached to a federal program.

For employment and regulatory counsel, the distinction matters even if the underlying worker protections stay in place. A ruling about who can decide the case can change how an enforcement action begins, what procedures it follows and where the parties contest the evidence. The hearing opened that question; it did not answer it.
